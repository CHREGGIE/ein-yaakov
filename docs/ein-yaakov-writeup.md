## What is Ein Yaakov?

The Gemara is more than halachah. Woven through every masechta are its aggados: the stories,
parables, prayers and teachings about Hashem, the soul, Klal Yisrael and how a person should live.
Ein Yaakov gathers those aggados from across Shas into one sefer, masechta by masechta, so they can
be learned on their own.

It was compiled by Rabbi Yaakov ibn Chaviv, one of the Torah scholars expelled from Spain in 1492.
He had planned the work in Spain but lacked the seforim to complete it; in Salonika, where he
settled, he found full sets of the Talmud and its commentaries and began compiling in his old age.
He printed the first part before his passing, and his son, Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv (the Ralbach),
completed it. Later editions added the commentaries printed around the text today, and the sefer
has been reprinted many times since.
[Sources: OU, "Rabbi Yaakov ibn Chaviv"; Chabad.org, "What Is Ein Yaakov?". Check: first printing,
Salonika, c. 1516.]

For generations, Ein Yaakov was the sefer of the shul: learned in a chevra between Mincha and
Maariv, by scholars and working people alike.

## Why we learn it: the Rebbeim of Lubavitch

**The Alter Rebbe** wrote to his chassidim asking that every weekday, in the time between Mincha and
Maariv, they learn Ein Yaakov together in a group of ten, along with practical halachos from the
Shulchan Aruch. He explains there that most of the secrets of the Torah are hidden in its aggadah.
[Source: Tanya, Iggeres HaKodesh, Epistle 23. Check the exact wording and the Rebbe's footnotes on
this letter.]

**The Rebbe Rayatz** related that chassidim made a passage of Ein Yaakov part of their fixed daily
learning, alongside Mishnayos, a daf of Gemara, Tanya and a halachah in Kitzur Shulchan Aruch.
[Source: cited in Chabad.org, "What Is Ein Yaakov?". Find the original sicha or letter.]
