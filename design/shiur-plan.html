Bringing Rabbi Fogelman's Ein Yaakov shiur to more listeners

A weekly podcast for people who follow along, and an online library of every shiur for people who learn at their own pace. Here is how it will work, and what we need from each of you.

The plan

Two ways to learn, from one set of recordings

People learn differently. Some like to keep pace with the shiur each week. Others want to start a masechta from the beginning, or learn something other than what the shiur is on now. Since we already have years of recordings, we can serve both.

The weekly podcast

For people who follow the shiur week by week

  • One new shiur each week, after it's been reviewed
  • Arrives on its own in Apple Podcasts, Spotify and other apps
  • Starts with the shiurim from launch onward, so listeners aren't greeted by years of old episodes

The shiur library

For people who learn at their own pace

  • Every recorded shiur on its own website, including the older ones that were never on a podcast
  • Browse by masechta, jump straight to a daf, or search for a topic or story
  • Each shiur has a summary, chapters and a full written transcript

How a shiur gets from the shul to listeners

  1. Recorded at the shulThe recording is saved to a shared Dropbox folder, one folder per masechta.
  2. Sound cleaned upRoom rumble and hum are reduced and the volume is evened out, gently enough that audience questions stay audible.
  3. TranscribedThe shiur is turned into text, which makes it searchable.
  4. Details draftedA draft title, daf range, short summary and chapter list are prepared from the transcript.
  5. Reviewed by a personSomeone listens and checks the details. Nothing goes public until this step is done.
  6. PublishedEvery reviewed shiur goes into the library. New shiurim also go out on the weekly podcast.
    Library: every shiurPodcast: new each week
For Rabbi Fogelman

A few decisions that are yours to make

Nothing will be made public until you've approved it. Until then the site and podcast stay private.
What should the shiur be called?

The name listeners will see in podcast apps and at the top of the site. The working name is "Ein Yaakov with Rabbi Fogelman". We'd also like a sentence or two describing the shiur in your words.

How should audience questions be handled?

Some recordings include questions from people in the room, and some of those voices are recognizable.

Keep them as they areTrim them outKeep them, but remove recognizable voices
Should the podcast avoid releasing on Shabbos and Yom Tov?

New episodes can be scheduled so they never appear on Shabbos or Yom Tov.

Who gives the final approval?

Whether you'd like to approve each shiur yourself, or have a trusted reviewer approve them and bring anything unusual to you.

How should each episode be titled?

The current suggestion is Ein Yaakov on Kesuvos – Shiur 5: Mar Ukva and hidden tzedakah. Happy to change it.

Dedications

Whether to offer dedications of individual shiurim (l'iluy nishmas or in honor of a simcha), and how they should be announced.

For the reviewer

What to check before a shiur goes public

For each shiur you'll get the cleaned-up audio along with its draft title, daf range, summary and chapters. The weekly shiur always comes first. Older recordings for the library can be reviewed in batches, one masechta at a time. This is a proposed checklist; tell us what to add or drop.
